## Block 1 — The case study

---

### Yauvana Estate

**Premium plotted land · Thirthahalli, Malnad**
`Self-initiated project`

---

**The problem**

Land buyers in Malnad cannot see what they are buying.

Plots are sold from a paper layout and a site visit that eats a whole day. Most
property sites in the region are a photo gallery and a phone number. They show
the land as it is today — trees, mud, a boundary stone. They do not show what it
becomes.

So the buyer is asked to imagine, and then to trust. Most of them do neither.

---

**The decision**

Photographs were the wrong proof.

You cannot photograph a house that has not been built. For plotted land, the
product is the future, not the present — which means renders and a 3D model are
the honest way to show it, not decoration on top of photos.

That one decision shaped the whole build.

---

**What was built**

- A scroll-driven image sequence that moves through the estate as you read,
  so the land reveals itself instead of sitting in a gallery
- An interactive 3D layout model — buyers can turn the layout and look at it
  from any angle, instead of squinting at a flat PDF
- An enquiry form wired to deliver straight to the seller, no dashboard,
  no middleman
- Motion built with GSAP and Lenis, so scrolling feels considered rather
  than mechanical
- Built, deployed and hosted end to end

---

**What it proves**

Everything above is running, not mocked up. The 3D model, the scroll sequence
and the motion are the parts most local studios will not attempt, and they are
the parts that make a buyer stay long enough to enquire.

The estate is a concept. The craft is not.

exposes it, and the damage is not the lie — it is that everything else you
said becomes suspect.

"Photographs were the wrong proof, so we built the future instead" is the
reason someone hires you. Clients cannot judge your code. They can judge
your thinking.

> businesses — real ones, with real customers — were invisible online. A
> brilliant cafe with no website. A builder whose only proof was a WhatsApp
> photo album.

> is the thing that makes someone pick you before they have met you.
>

**Why this works:** the cafe and the builder make it concrete. People do not
remember arguments, they remember examples.

---

## The photo

Put a real photo of your face in this section. Not the logo. Not an illustration.

For a solo studio in India, the face is the single strongest trust signal you
have. Business owners hire people, not brands, and a page with no human on it
reads as either a large faceless company or a person with something to hide.

- Plain background, a wall is fine
- Daylight, facing a window
- Looking at the camera
- Phone camera is fine
- Not a group photo, not sunglasses, not a crop from a wedding picture

Put your name under it.

---

## "We" or "I"

You have used "we" everywhere else. Keep it.

But About is where a client looks to find out who they would actually be
dealing with, so this is the one section where a name and a face appear. That
combination reads as "a small studio run by this person", which is exactly what
you are.

The rule: **never claim a team size.** Do not write "our team of designers".
Say "we build", show one face, use your name. Nobody has ever been upset by
that. People get upset when they expected five people and got one.

---

## What is still missing

About has no proof in it, and that is fine — proof lives in the Work section
above it. But two things would make this section much stronger, and both need
you to go get them:

**One line about why you can be trusted to do this.** Not a claim. A fact.
Something like the number of things you have built, or a specific skill most
local studios do not have. You have 3D and scroll work. That is a real,
checkable difference. One sentence.

**A testimonial, placed right under About.** Two sentences from a real person
with their name and business. The single highest-value thing you can collect
this week, and the thing About is currently missing most.

---

## Brand structure — a note for you, not for the page

0400 is the parent. Webessy Studios sits under it. The logo already shows this,
so the copy does not need to.

**Do not explain the hierarchy on the About page.** A studio with one person and
no clients describing its corporate structure reads as overcompensating. Say
"Webessy", let the logo carry the rest, and spell the structure out later when
there are genuinely two things under 0400.

The number is never explained anywhere on the site. It is a mark, not a story.

One practical thing: the largest element in your logo is `0400`, but the word
people say out loud is "Webessy". Expect to introduce yourself as Webessy.

## Rules for yourself

**Answer every message the same day, even if the answer is "give me till
tomorrow."** Silence is the only unforgivable thing in this business.

**Never send a follow-up more than twice.** If someone goes quiet after two
messages, let them go. They will come back or they will not, and chasing costs
you the referral.

**Save every conversation.** Even the ones that go nowhere. In six months those
messages will tell you exactly what people actually ask for, and that becomes
your services page.

**About putting your personal number on a public site.** You will get spam
calls and junk WhatsApp messages. That is the cost, and for a freelancer in
India it is usually worth paying, because a number gets you far more real
enquiries than a form does. If it gets bad, a second SIM for business is the
fix — not hiding the number.

**Switch to a WhatsApp Business account.** Free, same number, and you get a
business profile, away messages, and quick replies. Ten minutes, and it makes
you look like a company instead of a person with a phone.

# 7. Search and sharing

Mechanical stuff. Fill it in once, forget it.

---

## Read this first

Search will not bring you clients for months. A new site with no backlinks does
not rank for "website design bangalore" — that page is full of studios who have
been at it for years.

What will bring you clients in the first six months:

1. **People sharing your link on WhatsApp**
2. **Google Business Profile**
3. **You messaging people directly**

So the single most important thing in this file is not the meta description.
It is the **share image** — what people see when your link is pasted into a
WhatsApp chat. Get that right before anything else here.

---

## The share image (Open Graph)

When someone sends `webessy.com` in WhatsApp, a preview card appears. If you do
not set this, it shows nothing, or a random crop, and your link looks broken.

**Make one image, 1200 × 630 pixels:**

- Your logo on the dark background
- The line "Make people choose you."
- Nothing else. It renders small.

Save it as `og-image.jpg`, under 300 KB.

```html
<meta property="og:title" content="Webessy Studios">
<meta property="og:description" content="Websites that make people choose you.">
<meta property="og:image" content="https://webessy.com/og-image.jpg">
<meta property="og:url" content="https://webessy.com">
<meta property="og:type" content="website">
<meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image">
```

**Test it before launch.** Send the link to yourself on WhatsApp. If the preview
looks wrong, it is wrong for every client you ever send it to.

---

That is a job for version 2, once you have real projects in each category.
Do not build empty pages now to chase keywords — Google ignores thin pages and
they make your site look padded.
